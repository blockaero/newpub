Block Aero and AFRA Host US-China USM Sales Seminar in Phoenix

Industry leaders gathered to chart the future of cross-border USM sales between the US and China.

Block Aero and the Aircraft Fleet Recycling Association (AFRA) hosted a seminar in Phoenix focused on the future of used serviceable material sales between the United States and China. Industry leaders gathered to examine the opportunity — and the obstacles — in cross-border USM trade.

The opportunity is real. Aircraft retirements continue to release large volumes of serviceable material into the market, and demand for that material spans both regions. The challenge has rarely been the parts themselves; it has been trust in the records that accompany them. When material crosses a border, the documentation expectations of one jurisdiction do not always translate cleanly to another, and that gap slows transactions and raises the cost of verification.

The seminar brought operators, recyclers, and parts traders together to discuss how a shared, verifiable approach to traceability could narrow that trust gap. Recording a part's recovery and history at the source — and making that record consistently checkable — emerged as a practical path toward smoother cross-border sales.

Those conversations seeded the program that followed. The groundwork laid in Phoenix pointed toward registries that could capture teardown and component data in a durable, shared form, giving parties on both sides of a transaction a common reference for provenance.

That direction became today's AFRA-CAAC Registry, in which Block Aero serves as implementation partner — extending the seminar's central idea into a working framework for compliant traceability of material destined for China-registered aircraft.
