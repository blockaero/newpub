ecube + Unical Commit to the Next 100 Aircraft in the AFRA-CAAC Program

Scaling participation in compliant cross-border USM traceability under the AFRA-CAAC Registry program.

Block Aero is marking a milestone in the AFRA-CAAC Registry program: ecube and Unical have committed to the next 100 aircraft projects, scaling their participation in compliant cross-border used serviceable material traceability.

Block Aero serves as the implementation partner of the AFRA-CAAC Registry and operates the only overseas compliant parts management platform for the program, which is in a voluntary participation phase under CAAC Advisory Circular AC-145-FS-017 R1. The registries cover aircraft (MSN), engine (ESN), and dismantled parts (PART), with the goal of securing traceability from teardown to serviceable material that can be installed on China-registered aircraft.

A commitment at the scale of 100 aircraft is significant because it moves the program from individual proof-of-concept projects toward sustained, repeatable participation. Each aircraft that enters the program is registered by MSN, its engines tracked by ESN, and its harvested components recorded at the PART level — building a verified chain from the disassembly line forward.

For ecube and Unical, the value is in trust at the border. Cross-border USM sales have long carried documentation friction, where material recovered in one jurisdiction must satisfy the records expectations of another. Recording teardown and component data on the registries gives downstream parties a consistent, verifiable basis for evaluating material destined for China-registered fleets.

It is worth being precise about what the registry produces. A Dismantle Parts Tag generated through the program documents the harvest and its traceability; it is not an Authorized Release Certificate. The registries establish provenance and chain of custody — the foundation on which compliant transactions are built.
