Disassembly & Teardown Specialists

Every Removed Part, Traceable from the Cut

Value walks off a teardown one part at a time — and each one is only worth what its provenance can prove. Block Aero tracks the provenance of every part removed from aircraft and engine disassembly projects, applying the best practices of the Aircraft Fleet Recycling Association (AFRA) in both documentation and process.

Coordinate the harvest, end to end

Asset owners and the disassembly MROs performing the work coordinate the whole harvest in one place:

  • Issue and coordinate the harvest list between owner and facility.
  • Auto cross-reference against technical and regulatory instructions for continued airworthiness.
  • Capture and match teardown-manifest entries to the part marking or data-plate photo.
  • Live-share the harvest manifest, inspection images, and attached trace documentation.

Compliance built in — AFRA & CAAC

Integrated compliance with the regulatory and quality frameworks of AFRA and the CAAC — including the AFRA-CAAC aircraft (MSN), engine (ESN), and dismantled-parts (PART) registries — so projects and harvested material publish the mandatory removal information, photography, and certificates, right down to the CCAR-145–compliant removal tag.

Regulators & Authorities

Oversight You Stand Inside, Not a Snapshot You're Sent

What can Block Aero do for civil aviation authorities and regulators? Replace the reporting lag with a live system of record — and hold real authority over it without building the infrastructure yourself.

Digital Asset Manager

A regulator's picture of its fleet is only as current as the last paper filing. Digital Asset Manager replaces that lag with a live view: every aircraft, engine, and part in the jurisdiction, with compliance and configuration status written to a shared ledger by the operators, MROs, and disassembly facilities themselves. Oversight stops being a snapshot the industry sends you and becomes a system you're standing inside.

AI Records Manager

Enforcement lives or dies on documents: the tag, the release certificate, the teardown record. ARM reads them at the source — including the smudged, handwritten, and inconsistent ones — and turns them into structured data checked against the ledger, so a discrepancy surfaces the day it's created, not the year an inspector finds it. Fraud gets caught at document intake, while there's still something to do about it.

Registry as a Service

Regulators are asked to guarantee record integrity without the budget or mandate to run national IT infrastructure. Registry as a Service splits the job cleanly: an industry association operates the registry, the regulator holds the Auditor role with full inspection rights, Block Aero maintains the rails — and a free public lookup lets any buyer or insurer verify an asset without petitioning anyone. Full authority over the system of record, zero obligation to build or maintain it.
