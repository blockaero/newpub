Block Aero and Ascent Aviation Partner on Digital Aircraft Disassembly

Arizona-based Ascent Aviation Services is recording part harvests at the source on the Aviation Blockchain Network.

Block Aero and Ascent Aviation Services are partnering to bring digital disassembly and blockchain integration to teardown operations at Ascent's Arizona facilities. The goal is straightforward: capture each part's record at the moment it is harvested, rather than reconstructing that history later from paper.

Under the partnership, harvests are recorded at the source on the Aviation Blockchain Network. As components are removed during disassembly, they are entered into Block Aero's Digital Asset Manager and issued a digital passport — a verified, on-chain record that travels with the part from teardown forward.

Source-of-truth at disassembly matters because of where these parts go next. Used serviceable material moves through many hands before it is installed again, and every transfer is an opportunity for records to drift away from the physical asset. Recording the harvest at the source establishes a clean starting point: who removed the part, from which asset, and when. Downstream buyers, repair stations, and installers can trace back to that origin instead of trusting a chain of re-keyed documents.

For Ascent, the integration is also a customer-experience improvement. Operators sourcing material from an Ascent teardown gain immediate, verifiable visibility into a part's provenance, which shortens the documentation review that normally slows USM transactions.

The partnership reflects a broader pattern across the network: the earlier a part enters a digital record, the more its history can be trusted. Disassembly is the first link in that chain, and capturing it digitally at the source is where downstream confidence begins.
