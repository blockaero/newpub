Every Party Works from the Same Verified Truth

Aviation already operates as a network — your assets move between operators, shops, lessors, and regulators every day. So the ABN puts airlines, MROs, OEMs, lessors, regulators, and recyclers on one permissioned network of verified organizations, each with clear roles and responsibilities — and one shared record per asset. Blockchain Aviation Assets

A Tamper-Proof Digital Passport for Every Asset

Your assets will outlive their owners, their operators, and the IT systems that hold their records — and every change of hands risks breaking the paper trail. So every aircraft, engine, and serialized component on the network is issued a Blockchain Aviation Asset (BAA): a digital passport that:

  • Preserves the complete Back-to-Birth (BTB) chain of custody.
  • Consolidates identity, certifications, and maintenance records.
  • Survives ownership changes, company closures, and IT migrations.

Every BAA adds to a growing, trusted database of aviation asset records — building the shared, verifiable history the industry can finally rely on.

Data Sharing Specification

The Common Language for Aviation Asset Data

Everyone in aviation records the same facts, and everyone records them differently — which is why exchanging data still means spreadsheets, rework, and interpretation. The DSS therefore defines one set of rules and schemas for how asset data is structured, validated, and exchanged, so every digital passport is built with the same fields, codes, and permissions. Human-readable, machine-ready, and standards-driven.

Asset Profiles

Master data models for aircraft, engines, APUs (MSN/ESN/APU profiles), and serialized parts — covering identity, type certificates, states of design, manufacture, registry, and operation.

Roles & Permissions

Every field carries a read/write matrix across organization roles: OEMs hold write authority for design data, regulators read all compliance, operators write usage and maintenance fields.

Documents & Data Blocks

Standardized on-chain records — ADs, service bulletins, installation/removal records, release certificates, conformity certificates, non-incident statements — portable, versioned, and traceable.

Trust List of Organizations

Verified, Accredited, Continuously Screened

Knowing your counterparty is everything in this industry — but certificates expire, companies change hands, and most verification happens once and goes stale. So the ABN runs on a continuously screened Trust List (TL) of organizations, supported by a Trust Information List (TIL) that jointly define trusted sources and data sharing mechanisms — aligned with the ICAO Trust Framework and backed by Block Aero's ISO/IEC 27001:2022-certified information security management system (ASA CB, BLOC-001-06-26-27).

ComponentWhat it does
Trust List (TL)Organizations that have passed KYC checks, validated against authoritative sources (ICAO operator codes, FAA Repair Station database, ASA accreditation list). Records include legal names, codes, certificates, and status.
Trust Information List (TIL)Defines what information is exchanged on the network, and how.
Organization TypesNetwork Authorities (regulators, OEMs, associations) and Network Members (airlines, MROs, lessors, distributors) — each with distinct data responsibilities and permissions.
KYC ScreeningAll organizations undergo Know-Your-Customer screening via trusted third-party tools, with ongoing rescreening for ownership changes, sanctions, or dissolution.
Public PortalNon-members can view a subset of Trust List data — names, codes, websites, certificates — to increase transparency and industry confidence.
