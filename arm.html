What It Is

From a Paper Estate to a Machine-Checkable Record

AI Records Manager is an Early Access application on the Block Aero platform. It ingests the records an asset has accumulated over its life — however they arrive — and produces a structured data package: each document classified, its key fields extracted, its part identity cross-checked, and its gaps flagged. The output is built to the Data Sharing Specification and bound to the asset's digital passport, so the record is verifiable, not just stored.

Reads what you actually have

Structures it to the standard

Every document is classified against the 108-type Data Sharing Specification, so records mean the same thing to the party sending them and the party receiving them.

Keeps a human in the loop

AI does the heavy lifting; your team reviews and approves. Nothing is committed to an asset's passport without a person signing off.

The Records Problem

The Value Is in the Paperwork — and the Paperwork Is a Mess

Records gaps stall lease redeliveries for months, ground USM sales, and trigger re-certification costs that can exceed the value of the part. Yet onboarding a legacy estate into any digital system is a manual, line-by-line slog — the reason so many assets still trade with a folder of PDFs and a leap of faith.

  • Volume — a single airframe can carry tens of thousands of pages across its life.
  • Inconsistency — every operator, shop, and owner filed things differently.
  • Quality — scans, faxes-of-faxes, and tags that are barely legible.
  • Stakes — a break in back-to-birth traceability can scrap a life-limited part outright.
How It Works

Ingest, Classify, Extract, Verify, Structure

AI Records Manager runs the same discipline a receiving inspector applies to a box of paperwork — at machine speed, with a person confirming the calls that matter.

  • Step 1 · Ingest

    Take the records as they are

    Scanned PDFs, photos of tags, and mixed-quality documents go in without pre-sorting — no clean data feed required.

  • Step 2 · Classify

    Sort every document to the standard

    Each page is identified — FAA Form 8130-3, EASA Form 1, CAAC AAC-038, certificate of conformity, LLP status sheet, AD/SB compliance record, shop report, task card — and mapped to the 108-type Data Sharing Specification.

  • Step 3 · Extract

    Lift the fields that matter

    Part and serial numbers, CAGE codes, TSN/CSN, dates, and approvals are read from the page, not rekeyed by hand.

  • Step 4 · Verify

    Cross-check identity and find the gaps

    Each release certificate is cross-referenced against the records that came before it — do the 8130-3's P/N and S/N carry unbroken through the trace chain? — and breaks in back-to-birth traceability are flagged for review.

  • Step 5 · Structure

    Build the verifiable data package

    Approved records are assembled into a DSS-structured data package and anchored to the asset's digital passport — machine-checkable, shareable, and ready for a #RegistryReady submission.

Documents it classifies

  • FAA Form 8130-3 — authorized release certificate
  • EASA Form 1 and CAAC AAC-038 release certificates
  • Certificates of conformity
  • Life-limited part (LLP) status sheets
  • AD/SB compliance records
  • Shop reports, work orders, and task cards

Fields it extracts

  • Part numbers and serial numbers
  • CAGE codes and approval references
  • TSN/CSN — time and cycles since new
  • Release, cure, and inspection dates
  • Authority and organization identifiers
  • Trace-chain links between documents
