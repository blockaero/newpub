The Program

What the AFRA-CAAC Registry Is

As the world's retired-aircraft fleet grows, so does the value locked in their reusable parts — and the need to prove those parts are what they claim to be. The Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) addresses this in its Advisory Circular on aircraft disassembly (AC-145-FS-017 R1), which standardizes how disassembly organizations recover parts for return to service and keeps that return-to-service controllable, so non-conforming material never reaches a China-registered aircraft.

The challenge

Disassembly for return to service is, in essence, a maintenance activity — but parts recovered overseas have historically arrived with inconsistent paperwork and no shared, verifiable record of where they came from.

The framework

Disassembly organizations are managed under CCAR-145 principles, with airframe and engine ratings. The CAAC executed a Memorandum of Understanding with AFRA so that qualified overseas organizations can participate through AFRA's Best Management Practice (BMP).

The registry

Recovered parts and their source aircraft are recorded in a disassembled-part registration system — creating a traceable, queryable record that buyers, owners, and authorities can rely on.

How It Works

From Retired Airframe to Installed, Verified Part

Each step produces a record that travels with the part — so the proof is built once, at the source, and trusted everywhere after.

  • Step 1 · Disassembly

    Qualified organization recovers the part

    A CCAR-145-rated organization — or an overseas organization qualified through AFRA under the BMP — performs the disassembly for return to service.

  • Step 2 · Tagging

    An Aircraft Dismantle Parts Tag is issued

    The part is tagged with its name, part number/model, manufacturer, and serial number. Important: this tag attests that disassembly was performed correctly — it is not an Authorized Release Certificate.

  • Step 3 · Registration

    The part and its source aircraft are registered

    Registration captures the aircraft type, manufacturer serial number, the nationality registration mark held before retirement, the airworthiness certificate, and a copy of the Certificate of Deregistration — with each part's dismantle tag attached. This is recorded on a qualifying registration platform.

  • Step 4 · Verification

    Buyers verify before they commit

    Airlines, MROs, and distributors query the registry to confirm a part's provenance and eligibility — turning weeks of due diligence into minutes.

  • Step 5 · Return to Service

    Release certification completes the chain

    For return to service, a Maintenance Release / Authorized Release Certificate (e.g. AAC-038 / ARC) is issued by an appropriately rated organization. The registry record links the full history end to end.

Three Registries, One Chain of Custody

Aircraft, Engines, and Dismantled Parts

The program spans three connected registries on Block Aero, so a part can be traced all the way back to the airframe it came from.

Aircraft Registry · MSN Engine Registry · ESN Dismantled Parts Registry · PART

Each registry entry holds the records that make a part trustworthy:

  • Aircraft type, manufacturer serial number, and pre-retirement nationality registration mark.
  • Airworthiness Certificate and Certificate of Deregistration.
  • A copy of each part's Aircraft Dismantle Parts Tag, with part number, manufacturer, and serial number.

Know the Difference

The Dismantle Parts Tag vs. the Release Certificate

A common point of confusion — and one the registry makes unambiguous.

DocumentWhat it attestsEnables return to service?
Aircraft Dismantle Parts TagThe disassembly was carried out correctly by a qualified organization, and the part is identified (name, P/N, manufacturer, S/N).No — on its own
Authorized Release Certificate (e.g. AAC-038 / ARC)The part has been found airworthy and is approved for return to service by an appropriately rated organization.Yes

The registry links both to the same part record, so buyers always know exactly which approvals a part carries — and which it still needs.

Who Participates

A Coordinated Program

CAAC

Sets the framework through the Advisory Circular and recognizes qualifying registration platforms.

AFRA

Under an MOU with the CAAC, qualifies overseas organizations through its Best Management Practice (BMP).

Disassembly Organizations

CCAR-145-rated and AFRA-qualified overseas organizations recover parts, tag them, and register them.

Asset Owners

Provide aircraft provenance — deregistration, airworthiness, and configuration records — to anchor each registry entry.

Buyers — Airlines & MROs

Verify eligibility and provenance against the registry before parts are installed on China-registered aircraft.
