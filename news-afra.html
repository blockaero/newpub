Block Aero and AFRA Partner on Used Parts Registries

Simplifying trace compliance for used serviceable material with blockchain-backed registries.

Block Aero and the Aircraft Fleet Recycling Association (AFRA) are partnering to simplify trace compliance for used serviceable material through blockchain-backed registries. The collaboration brings AFRA's recycling and disassembly expertise together with Block Aero's verified-records platform.

AFRA sets the industry's recognized best practices for aircraft and engine disassembly and parts recovery. Material harvested under those practices already carries an expectation of careful handling and documentation — but historically that documentation has lived in paper files and disconnected systems, making trace compliance slow and difficult to verify as parts change hands.

Blockchain-backed registries change the starting point. By recording a part's recovery and traceability data at the source, the registry creates a verified record that can be referenced rather than reconstructed. Downstream buyers, repair stations, and operators can check provenance against a consistent record instead of assembling a documentation trail from scratch.

The aim is not to add paperwork but to reduce it — to make the trace history of used serviceable material easier to capture once and trust thereafter. For an industry where a part may be removed, stored, repaired, sold, and installed across several owners and jurisdictions, a durable record at the point of recovery removes a recurring source of friction.

This partnership laid the foundation for what became the AFRA-CAAC Registry — the program in which Block Aero serves as implementation partner, extending the same registry approach to compliant cross-border traceability for material destined for China-registered aircraft.
