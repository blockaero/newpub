The Aviation Records Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the records, USM, and platform terms you'll meet across aviation asset management.

A – F

Records & Asset Terms

AD/SB Compliance (Airworthiness Directive / Service Bulletin Compliance)

An Airworthiness Directive (AD) is a legally enforceable requirement issued by a civil aviation authority — such as the FAA or EASA — mandating an inspection, repair, modification, or operating limitation to correct an unsafe condition in an aircraft, engine, or part. A Service Bulletin (SB) is a manufacturer-issued recommendation that becomes mandatory only when a regulator incorporates it into an AD; AD/SB compliance records document which directives and bulletins apply to an asset and show that the required actions have been completed.

AFRA (Aircraft Fleet Recycling Association)

An international association that develops best practices for the safe disassembly of end-of-life aircraft and engines and the responsible recovery of parts and materials. AFRA accredits facilities against its standards and is a partner in the AFRA-CAAC Registry program.

AFRA BMP (Best Management Practice)

AFRA's published guidance and accreditation standard for aircraft disassembly and parts/material recycling, covering how parts are removed, tagged, and documented at teardown. Facilities accredited to the AFRA BMP follow a consistent, auditable process for end-of-life work.

AFRA-CAAC Registry

A registry of disassembled-parts records created when aircraft are torn down at accredited facilities, supporting cross-border used-parts traceability between the US and China. The program operates in a voluntary participation phase under CAAC Advisory Circular AC-145-FS-017 R1; Block Aero is the implementation partner of the AFRA-CAAC Registry and the only overseas compliant parts management platform. Learn more about the program.

Authorized Release Certificate (FAA Form 8130-3 / EASA Form 1 / CAAC AAC-038)

An airworthiness release document issued by an approved organization to certify that a part has been produced or maintained in conformity with approved data and is eligible for installation or return to service. The FAA Form 8130-3, EASA Form 1, and CAAC AAC-038 are the equivalent forms used in their respective jurisdictions.

Aviation Blockchain Network (ABN)

The shared, permissioned network Block Aero operates so that approved aviation organizations can record and exchange asset records as tamper-evident, time-stamped entries. The ABN is the foundation beneath Digital Passports, the Trust List, and the platform's data-sharing features. See how the network works.

Back-to-Birth (BTB) Traceability

A complete, unbroken records trail for a part from its original manufacture through every install, removal, repair, and ownership change to the present day. Back-to-birth traceability is typically required to establish the remaining life and value of life-limited and other tracked parts.

Blockchain Aviation Asset (BAA) / Digital Passport

Block Aero's digital record for an individual asset — an aircraft, engine, or part — that gathers its identity, documents, and history into a single verifiable profile on the network. The BAA, or Digital Passport, lets an owner share proof of an asset's records without handing over the originals. Explore Digital Passports.

CAAC (Civil Aviation Administration of China)

The civil aviation authority of the People's Republic of China, responsible for regulating airworthiness, operations, and maintenance organizations within China. CAAC issues the advisory circulars and forms (such as AAC-038) referenced in the AFRA-CAAC Registry program.

CAGE (Commercial and Government Entity) Code

A five-character alphanumeric code assigned by the U.S. Defense Logistics Agency to identify a specific manufacturer, distributor, or other entity doing business with the U.S. government. CAGE codes appear throughout aviation records and release documents to identify who produced or certified a part; the international equivalent is the NATO Commercial and Government Entity (NCAGE) code.

CCAR-145

The China Civil Aviation Regulation governing approved maintenance organizations — the CAAC equivalent of FAA Part 145 and EASA Part-145. A CCAR-145 approval authorizes an organization to perform and release specified maintenance on aircraft and components.

Certificate of Conformity (CoC)

A supplier's or manufacturer's written statement that a part, material, or shipment conforms to the applicable specification, drawing, or purchase order. A Certificate of Conformity is common for raw material, standard parts, and consumables, but — unlike an Authorized Release Certificate — it does not by itself constitute an airworthiness release.

Chain of Custody

The documented sequence of who held, handled, or owned a part and its records over time. A clear chain of custody helps a buyer or inspector trust that records belong to the part in front of them and have not been substituted or lost.

Data Package Creation

Assembling the documents and records for an asset — certificates, trace, and history — into an organized, shareable package for a sale, audit, or return to service. Block Aero supports Data Package Creation so the right records can be presented and verified together.

Data Sharing Specification (DSS)

Block Aero's structured specification for organizing and exchanging asset records consistently across organizations, scoped to 108 document types. The DSS gives the network a common vocabulary so records mean the same thing to the party sending them and the party receiving them. See the Data Sharing Specification.

Digital Asset Manager

Block Aero's core product (offered on a monthly or annual subscription) for digitizing, organizing, and securely sharing the records behind your aviation assets. It is the day-to-day home for an organization's Digital Passports and document packages. Explore Digital Asset Manager.

Dismantle Parts Tag

A tag generated when a part is removed during disassembly that records the part's identity and the teardown event for traceability purposes. A Dismantle Parts Tag is not an Authorized Release Certificate — it does not replace an FAA Form 8130-3, EASA Form 1, or CAAC AAC-038 and does not by itself make a part eligible for installation.

Dual Release

A part, repair, or maintenance action released under airworthiness documentation that two civil aviation authorities recognize at the same time — for example, a repair station holding both FAA and EASA certification that issues a release accepted under both systems through their bilateral agreement. Dual release lets an operator use the part or the completed work in either jurisdiction without a separate validation step, provided the underlying approvals and agreement are in place.

EOL (End of Life) Aircraft / Engine

An aircraft or engine that has been retired from service and is destined for disassembly, parts recovery, and recycling rather than continued operation. End-of-life assets are the source of much of the industry's used serviceable material.

ESN (Engine Serial Number)

The unique serial number assigned by the manufacturer to an individual engine, used to track that engine's records, time, and cycles throughout its life. Example format: ESN-733142.

FSCAP (Flight Safety Critical Aircraft Part)

A part whose failure could cause loss of the aircraft or a catastrophic outcome, and which is therefore subject to heightened traceability and handling controls. FSCAP status raises the bar for the records that must accompany the part.

G – O

Trust, Identity & Marketplace Terms

ICAO Trust Framework

A model promoted by the International Civil Aviation Organization for establishing trusted, secure exchange of aviation data between parties and systems. It informs how digital identity and verifiable records can be shared safely across organizations and borders.

ILS (Marketplace)

Inventory Locator Service, a long-established online marketplace where the aviation industry lists and sources parts, components, and services. Sellers using Block Aero's openAPI Integration Services can attach immutable links to on-chain proof of ownership when listing assets on marketplaces such as ILS.

Lease Redelivery

The process of returning a leased aircraft or engine to its lessor at the end of, or upon early termination of, a lease — in the technical condition and with the supporting records specified in the lease agreement. Redelivery typically requires demonstrating AD/SB compliance and life-limited part status and assembling a complete records package, often verified through a joint inspection between lessee and lessor.

LLP (Life-Limited Part)

A part with a mandatory maximum operating life — usually expressed in flight cycles or hours — after which it must be removed from service and cannot be overhauled back into use. Accurate back-to-birth records are essential to prove how much life an LLP has remaining.

MSN (Manufacturer Serial Number / Aircraft)

The unique serial number assigned by the manufacturer to an individual airframe, fixed for the life of the aircraft regardless of registration changes. Example format: MSN-29481.

openAPI Integration Services

Block Aero's set of APIs that let external systems and marketplaces connect to the network — for example to publish or verify proof of ownership programmatically. They allow partners to weave verified records into their own workflows. See the integrations.

P – Z

Ownership, Product & Standards Terms

PART (Dismantled Part Registry Record)

The registry record that represents an individual dismantled part recovered during disassembly, capturing its identity and teardown details for traceability. A PART record documents provenance; it is not an airworthiness release and does not replace an Authorized Release Certificate.

Proof of Ownership

Verifiable evidence on the network that a given organization holds title to an asset at a point in time, which can be shared with buyers or marketplaces without exposing the underlying documents. It lets a counterparty confirm ownership in minutes rather than chasing paperwork.

Receiving Inspection

The process of examining an incoming part, material, or shipment — matching it against its accompanying release certificate or conformity documentation and checking its physical condition and markings — before it is accepted into stock or approved for use. Receiving inspection is the standard checkpoint for catching missing paperwork, damage, or a suspected unapproved part before it enters inventory.

Registry Manager / Registry as a Service

Block Aero's add-on product — Registry as a Service — for organizations that need to operate a structured registry of asset or parts records, such as a disassembly registry. It extends the core Digital Asset Manager with registry-specific capabilities. Explore Registry Manager.

SUP (Suspected Unapproved Part)

A part, component, or material that is suspected — but not yet confirmed — of not conforming to its approved design, or of lacking the production, maintenance, or documentation required to be eligible for installation. A suspected unapproved part is typically quarantined and investigated before a final determination is made, and, if confirmed, is reported and removed from the supply chain.

Trust List

The curated list of approved organizations and identities recognized on the Aviation Blockchain Network, so that participants know who they are exchanging records with. The Trust List underpins confidence in every record's origin. See the Trust List.

TSN/CSN (Time Since New / Cycles Since New)

TSN (Time Since New) is the total flight hours an aircraft, engine, or part has accumulated since it was originally manufactured; CSN (Cycles Since New) is the total number of flight cycles — one takeoff and landing — accumulated over that same period. Both figures are foundational to tracking a life-limited part's remaining service life and to scheduling time- or cycle-based maintenance.

USM (Used Serviceable Material)

Parts and materials that have been previously installed, removed in serviceable condition, and made available for reuse — typically harvested from end-of-life aircraft and engines. The value and saleability of USM depend heavily on the quality of its accompanying records. See USM solutions.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022

The international standard for information security management systems. Block Aero is certified to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 by the ASA Certification Bureau (ASA CB) under Certificate BLOC-001-06-26-27, issued 11 June 2026 and valid through 10 June 2029. Visit the Trust Center.
